Research

My research lies at the intersection of scientific computing and artificial intelligence, with a focus on building first principles into AI models to address complex challenges in science and engineering. My interdisciplinary approach bridges traditional numerical methods and modern machine learning.

Physics-informed machine learning

UC Riverside, 2026–present · with Maziar Raissi

I develop algorithms that combine data-driven methods with first principles such as the conservation of mass, energy, and momentum. Central to this work are physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), which embed physical laws, described by ordinary and partial differential equations, directly into their architecture.

This makes it possible to model and simulate complex systems where data and physics interact efficiently, producing solutions that are both accurate and consistent with the laws of nature. Applications include computational modeling, biomedical sciences, and engineering.

AI agents for scientific simulation

UC Riverside, 2026–present

I develop and train AI agents that assist with scientific simulation: setting up mathematical models, generating and running numerical solvers (for example, finite element codes in FEniCSx), and analyzing and validating the results. The goal is to make high-fidelity simulation faster, more accessible, and more reliable across science and engineering.

Mathematical models in continuum physics

Texas A&M University, 2025–2026 · with Arkadz Kirshtein

A comprehensive review of mathematical models in continuum physics covering diffusion, elasticity, phase-field models, liquid crystals, and non-isothermal systems, including coupled Navier–Stokes, Allen–Cahn, Poisson–Nernst–Planck, and chemically reactive systems with thermal effects.

A novel variational formulation of elasticity in an Eulerian framework using reference-map techniques, supported by numerical simulations.

Nonlinear integral equations in population dynamics

University of Houston, 2020–2025

Computational methods for nonlinear integral equations arising in population dynamics: a Galerkin method based on thin plate splines with composite Gauss–Legendre integration for stability and accuracy, and Legendre wavelet Galerkin schemes for models with immigration and time delay. Both are supported by error analysis and numerical validation.

Reaction–diffusion models of disease spread

University of Houston · with William E. Fitzgibbon

Positivity-preserving semilinear dissipative systems for the spatial spread of disease via advection–diffusion models, including analysis and numerical simulation of waterborne disease spread with dynamic boundaries.

Cahn–Hilliard simulation

Project · Python, FEniCSx

A finite element implementation of a Cahn–Hilliard phase-field model using Python and FEniCSx.

Machine learning for adverse drug events

Kansas State University, 2018–2020 · with Majid Jaberi-Douraki

Similarity association analysis of adverse drug events using AI and machine learning. Five measures (Pearson, Spearman, cosine, Yule, and Euclidean distance) were implemented in Python to identify associated events and filter noise.

Numerical methods for optimal control

Amirkabir University of Technology, 2015–2017 · with Mostafa Shamsi

Local methods with adaptive meshes for solving optimal control problems, including Euler and Hermite–Simpson methods, with applications to biology, economics, and finance.
